This is a short, honest look at the numbers behind Nigeria's private security sector — not a proprietary study, and not built from Lemtik's own customer data, since we're pre-pilot as a company. It's a snapshot of public, cited figures, updated as better numbers become available.

## The industry is large and growing

Nigeria's electronic security and surveillance market — cameras, access control, alarm systems — was valued at roughly **$80 million in 2025**, and is projected to grow at a compound annual rate of about **9.25%**, reaching an estimated **$136 million by 2031** ([Ken Research](https://www.kenresearch.com/industry-reports/nigeria-electronic-security-and-surveillance-market)).

That sits inside a much larger physical-security services sector. Nigeria has over **1,100 licensed private security firms** employing more than **one million corporate guards**, with the broader industry — including an estimated two million unlicensed guards and 750+ unlicensed operators — employing over **four million Nigerians** in total ([U.S. International Trade Administration, Nigeria Country Commercial Guide](https://trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/nigeria-safety-and-security)).

## Private security, not public, protects most of the country's assets

By some estimates, private security companies protect over **90% of the value of Nigeria's GDP-generating assets** — put at more than **$218 billion** — a reflection of how much of the country's actual security coverage runs through private firms rather than public policing capacity ([Guardian Nigeria](https://guardian.ng/?p=128476); [BusinessDay](https://businessday.ng/opinion/article/one-big-security-industry-nigerias-untapped-economic-powerhouse/)).

## The demand signal is real — and it's currently pointed at hardware

Search behavior backs this up directly: live classifieds listings for "guard tour patrol system" hardware — handheld NFC/QR scanning wands — are active in Lagos right now, priced from roughly **₦95,000–₦100,000**. That's genuine, present-tense buyer demand for patrol verification. It's just currently being met almost entirely by standalone hardware, not by connected software platforms (source: live Jiji.ng marketplace listings, observed October 2026).

## What the numbers don't capture

None of the sources above measure something that matters just as much: how much of this four-million-person industry is still coordinating day-to-day operations through phone calls, radio, and WhatsApp groups rather than a system that produces a real record. There's no public dataset for that — it's something we hear directly from estate managers, hotel security directors, and officers in the founder-led conversations that shaped this platform, not something we're claiming is independently measured.

That gap — a large, growing, economically significant industry, still running its daily coordination on tools that weren't built for it — is the specific problem Lemtik exists to close.
