Most security management software on the market was built for the US or Europe first, and adapted for everywhere else second. That shows up in ways that aren't obvious from a sales demo. Here's what to actually look at.

## Does it work when the internet doesn't?

Connectivity in Lagos, Abuja, or Port Harcourt is not the connectivity the vendor tested on. Ask directly: what happens when a guard's phone drops signal mid-patrol? Does the app queue the check-in and sync later, or does it just fail silently? This single question eliminates more vendors than any feature comparison will.

## Can it actually reach your guards?

A platform that assumes every user has a modern smartphone, fast data, and comfort with a complex app will quietly exclude a chunk of your actual security staff. Ask what the guard-facing experience requires — device, data usage per shift, literacy level — before you ask about the manager-facing dashboard, because the dashboard isn't where incidents get reported.

## Who actually owns the data, and where does it live?

Ask plainly where your data is stored and under which country's data protection law it's governed. The Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA) is the relevant framework for data generated in Nigeria — a vendor who can only talk about GDPR compliance is telling you their product wasn't built with this market as the starting point.

## What happens to the audit trail if someone disputes an incident?

If your security system is ever going to matter to an insurer, a court, or an internal investigation, the record needs to be something nobody — including your own staff, including an administrator — can quietly edit after the fact. Ask specifically: can any user, at any permission level, alter a submitted incident record? If the answer is "yes, with the right access," that's not an audit trail, it's a shared document.

## Does pricing assume your scale, or someone else's?

A lot of security software pricing is built around a US enterprise buyer's budget and headcount. Ask for pricing scoped to your actual site count and team size before you spend time on a feature walkthrough — if a vendor can't price for a single estate or a multi-site hospitality group without a six-week sales process, that tells you who they're actually built for.

## Does the vendor understand what you're protecting against?

Theft, trespassing, and fire are different problems from the ones a US enterprise security buyer is usually solving (cyber-physical convergence, workplace violence protocols tuned to US law). Ask the vendor to describe, specifically, the incident types their system was designed around. If the answer is generic, the product probably is too.

## The short version

Software built for a different market can still work here — but you should make the vendor prove it was built to, rather than assuming it was. Ask about offline behavior, device requirements, data residency, audit-trail integrity, and realistic pricing before you ask about anything else.
