"Command centre" gets attached to almost anything with a screen in it — a wall of CCTV monitors, a dashboard, even a well-organized spreadsheet. The term is specific enough to be worth defining properly, because the difference matters when you're deciding what you actually need.

## It's not just a place to watch things

A room full of camera feeds is surveillance. It becomes a command centre only when watching connects to *doing* — when whoever is in that room, or looking at that dashboard, can actually direct a response, not just observe one happening somewhere else.

## Three things a real command centre does

**It brings every source of information into one place.** Radio chatter, a resident's report, a camera alert, a patrol's GPS position — in most operations these all live in different places, checked by different people, on different schedules. A command centre's whole job is making all of it visible from one view, at the same time.

**It turns information into a decision.** Seeing an alert isn't the point. The point is knowing, fast, who's closest, who's available, and what the right response actually is — and having that answer be consistent regardless of which operator is on shift.

**It keeps a record of what was decided and why.** A command centre that doesn't log its own decisions is just a nicer-looking version of the same ad-hoc coordination it replaced. The audit trail isn't a reporting feature bolted on afterward — it's the reason the decisions made in that room hold up later, whether that's to an insurer, a board, or a regulator.

## What it isn't

It isn't necessarily a physical room. For a single estate, "command centre" might be one supervisor's phone running the right software — the concept scales down fine. It also isn't a replacement for people: the AI-assisted versions of this (including Lemtik's) are explicit that a human operator makes the actual call, with the system doing the work of surfacing the right information fast enough to make a good call possible.

## Why the distinction matters when you're buying

If a vendor's "command centre" is really just a CCTV viewer with a new coat of paint, you'll find out the hard way — during an actual incident, when you discover there's no way to coordinate a response from inside the tool you're paying for. Ask specifically: can an operator dispatch a response from this screen, or just watch one happen?
